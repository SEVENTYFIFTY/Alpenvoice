The Challenge

Swiss Youth Mental Health is Under Unprecedented Strain

The mental health of children and adolescents has become a priority topic in Switzerland. Increasing levels of psychological distress, capacity constraints in care provision, and pressure on educational and healthcare institutions highlight the urgent need for complementary support mechanisms.

“Preventive, early-intervention and low-threshold services remain underdeveloped, while the system continues to be predominantly curative.” — Swiss Health Report 2025
📈

Rising Mental Health Burdens

20–30% of Swiss children and adolescents experience low emotional well-being or medium to high psychological burden.

⏳

Long Waiting Times

Several months for assessments and treatment have become common, in some cases extending well beyond this.

🧑‍⚕️

Professional Shortages

A persistent shortage affects psychiatrists, psychologists, and therapeutic specialists across Switzerland.

🔍

Late Detection

Too late recognition of issues and communication problems between stakeholders are the most reported challenges.

Built Against Bias from Day One

Petrus AI never compares a child to other children. Every signal is measured against that child's own personal baseline over the previous four weeks.

A naturally quiet child is not flagged for being quiet — they are only flagged if they become significantly quieter than their own usual pattern.

This architectural decision means the system does not penalise children who are simply different from the average. A Portuguese child in a German-speaking school, a reserved child in an expressive classroom — their baseline is their own.

The Human Gate — Non-Negotiable by Design

No alert ever reaches a parent, teacher, or family directly from the system.

Every alert passes through a school psychologist first. She reviews it. She decides whether it warrants action. If she considers it a false alarm, it is dismissed — the family never knows, the child never knows.

The system proposes. The professional decides. Always.

Four-Level Alert Architecture

GREENScore 0.00–0.35

No action. Pattern logged for baseline tracking.

  • Visible to: System only
  • Family notified: Never
YELLOWScore 0.35–0.55

Early monitoring indicator. Psychologist notified for review.

  • Visible to: School psychologist
  • Family notified: Never
ORANGEScore 0.55–0.75

Psychologist reviews and schedules observation.

  • Visible to: Psychologist + school head
  • Family notified: Only if psychologist confirms
REDScore 0.75–1.00

Psychologist contacts parents. Clinical referral pathway opens.

  • Visible to: Full care team
  • Family notified: Yes — psychologist-mediated only

Minimum session threshold: A child with fewer than 20 sessions cannot receive an ORANGE or RED alert regardless of score. The system requires sufficient baseline history before issuing a serious flag.

Privacy & Regulatory Compliance

Designed for EU AI Act Article 13 compliance — transparency obligations for high-risk AI systems — ahead of 2026 enforcement, not as a retrofit.

Petrus AI is designed to inform, explain, normalize, and refer — not to diagnose or treat. This positioning minimizes regulatory complexity while maximizing value for institutions.

  • Inform, explain, and normalize mental health topics
  • Refer to appropriate professionals when needed
  • No diagnosis or treatment — minimal liability
  • Age-appropriate design for 6–11, 12–14, and 15–18 year olds
  • Data residency: Azure Switzerland North (Zürich), failover to Azure Switzerland West (Geneva)
  • Pseudonymisation: Petrus AI cannot re-identify a child — the re-identification key is held exclusively by the institution
  • Breach notification: 72 hours to Swiss FDPIC per nFADP Art. 24
  • Swiss nFADP, GDPR, and EU AI Act Article 13 (high-risk AI transparency) compliant — built-in from day one, not as a retrofit

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need to Know

Answers to common questions from investors, schools, and institutional partners about Petrus AI and our mission.

Product & Technology

What is Petrus AI and how does it work?

Petrus AI is a low-threshold, AI-supported digital platform for early detection of children's mental health risks. Nightly behavioral analysis measures each child against their own four-week personal baseline — never against other children — and delivers alerts to a school psychologist's queue each morning. A mandatory human review precedes any stakeholder notification.

What age groups does Petrus AI support?

Children and adolescents aged 6–18, through three distinct age-adaptive UX modes: 6–11, 12–14, and 15–18 — ensuring developmentally appropriate interactions at every age.

How is Petrus AI different from existing mental health apps?

Unlike wellness apps focused on meditation or adult-only prescription platforms, Petrus AI is purpose-built for institutions: AI-first early detection, school integration, a structured psychologist-gated referral pathway, Swiss 4-language support, and built-in Swiss/EU compliance — 10/10 critical capabilities versus a competitor average of 3–5.

What features do institutions rate as most important?

Early detection leads with 4.7/5, followed by communication support (4.6/5), decision support (4.5/5), and progress tracking (4.4/5) — based on our 33-institution survey across 8 Swiss cantons.

For Schools & Institutions

How can my school participate in the pilot program?

We're partnering with 1–2 beta schools under research agreements in Q3–Q4 2026, with first paid pilots launching Q1–Q2 2027. Contact us at info@seventyfifty.ch to discuss participation.

What challenges do institutions report most frequently?

Professional overload (94% of schools, 89% of psychiatry services), late detection (56% / 64%), communication problems (61% / 57%), and time constraints (44% / 71%).

What does implementation look like for a school?

The child-facing platform is available 24/7. Alert processing runs nightly with results delivered to the school psychologist's queue each morning. Every alert passes through the psychologist before any family or teacher is notified — the system proposes, the professional decides.

How is the platform priced for institutions?

Annual contracts: CHF 6,000 for schools up to 500 students, CHF 12,000 for schools of 500–1,500 students, CHF 18,000 for psychiatric clinics, and CHF 24,000 for hospital departments. Blended average contract value is roughly CHF 10,000 per year.

Privacy & Compliance

How does Petrus AI handle data privacy?

Data residency is Azure Switzerland North (Zürich) with failover to Azure Switzerland West (Geneva). Pseudonymisation by design means Petrus AI cannot re-identify a child — the re-identification key is held exclusively by the institution. Breach notification to the Swiss FDPIC within 72 hours per nFADP Art. 24.

Is Petrus AI classified as a medical device?

No. Petrus AI is designed to inform, explain, normalize, and refer — not to diagnose or treat. This positioning minimizes regulatory complexity and liability while maximizing value for institutions.

What about parental consent for children?

Deployment runs through institutions with appropriate consent frameworks. Families are only ever contacted through the school psychologist — never directly by the system — and a dismissed false alarm is never visible to the family or the child.

How does the system avoid false alarms with limited data?

A minimum session threshold: a child with fewer than 20 sessions cannot receive an ORANGE or RED alert regardless of score. The system requires sufficient baseline history before issuing a serious flag, and every alert is reviewed by a psychologist first.

How do you address concerns about AI and children?

Three structural safeguards: the personal-baseline architecture (no comparison to other children, built against bias from day one), the non-negotiable human gate (a professional always decides), and EU AI Act Article 13 transparency compliance designed in ahead of 2026 enforcement.

CHF 750,000 seed round at a CHF 5–7M pre-money valuation, structured in four milestone-based tranches: CHF 150K upon closing, two CHF 250K tranches on pilot agreements and user engagement milestones, and a final CHF 100K on research completion.

What are the expected returns?

Scenario analysis based on comparable European health/edtech SaaS acquisitions (4–8× ARR on a targeted CHF 33.6M ARR by 2031) implies exit values of CHF 134M–269M, or 13.4×–34.9× returns depending on stake and scenario. These are projections, not guarantees.

What is the market size opportunity?

~48,000 addressable institutions across DACH (~45,500 schools plus ~2,500 psychiatric institutions) serving 13.1M children aged 6–18. The market is growing at 24% CAGR, projected to reach €7B by 2030.

What traction do you have so far?

Two co-founders with technical leadership, 400+ research hours, 33 institutional survey responses across 8 cantons, and survey validation showing 14 Swiss psychiatric services ranked early detection as their highest-priority unmet need.

How will the funds be used?

45% product development (CHF 337K), 20% clinical validation (CHF 150K — minimum 500 enrolled children with independent audit), 20% market entry (CHF 150K), and 15% operations (CHF 113K).

Research & Validation

What research validates Petrus AI?

400+ hours of market research conducted with FH Graubünden under Prof. Dr. Ivan Nikitin, covering problem context, market analysis, legal compliance, and UX requirements — with 33 institutional responses across 8 Swiss cantons.

What did the institutional survey reveal?

89–93% of professionals report capacity constraints, early detection rated 4.7/5 as the most important feature need, and 75%+ of institutions open to adopting AI-supported digital tools.

What's the validation roadmap?

2026: clinical validation methodology designed with FH Graubünden and beta deployment. 2027: data accumulation across paid pilots. 2028: clinical validation milestone with a minimum of 500 enrolled children, school psychologist labeling, and independent review before any production deployment.

How was the research funded?

The research was conducted by two FH Graubünden student teams within the university's Project Management module, supervised by Prof. Dr. Ivan Nikitin — combining academic rigor with entrepreneurial sponsorship by Seventy Fifty.
